01 / OVERVIEW

A challenge set grounded
in online events and claims.

Does performance on existing benchmarks carry over to evidence-linked images encountered in actual online circulation?

WildAIGC targets photographic-looking AI-generated or generatively edited content linked to news events and social-media claims. Acquired files include reposts, publisher reproductions and screenshots. This is a curated challenge set, not a representative sample of all internet images.

WildAIGC-Bench focuses on realistic-looking synthetic content connected to documented events and claims. Its first release contains 407 human-accepted AI images, with source references, evidence summaries and explicit limitations.

The set is positive-only. It measures AI-image recall; a separate real-image reference is needed for false-positive rates or complete binary-classification metrics. Complete compression histories are not inferred.

RAW

The acquired online file.

Original collected bytes, including any existing comments, platform interfaces, identifiers, captions or generation marks. An acquired online file is not necessarily the generator’s earliest output.

PROCESSED

The region under examination.

Human-selected rectangular crops focus on the target image and remove surrounding comments, generation watermarks, platform identifiers or other cues where cropping permits. No inpainting, enhancement or resizing.

All 407 images were manually checked for crop suitability. Target regions were cropped where needed; otherwise the original image was retained. Crops are pixel-exact regions from raw, saved losslessly. Uncropped raw and processed files are byte-identical. Some cues can remain; processed does not mean completely free of leakage.
03 / CONSTRUCTION

From a circulating claim
to a frozen evaluation sample.

A documented workflow joins media discovery, provenance checks and human decisions, with a separate release admission gate.

Preserve the evidence

Record the acquired image, source URLs and available generative evidence. Fact-check conclusions and reported forensic tests remain attributed to their sources.

Separate collection from acceptance

Review image–claim correspondence, final AI labels and inclusion decisions. Screen duplicates, sensitive content, low-quality assets and unsuitable creative examples.

Keep the release traceable

Preserve raw bytes, group related samples, retain crop coordinates and freeze file hashes in a versioned manifest. Additional collection belongs to future releases.

Two reviewers. Context-based decisions.

Both reviewers checked every accepted image. Disagreements were resolved jointly using its context and source evidence; only consensus cases were retained. Evidence included creator declarations, explicit detection of generation-platform watermarks (such as SynthID or OpenAI-related signals), and fact-checking conclusions. These are the maintainers’ reported review procedures, not a claim that every forensic test was independently repeated.

Outputs of general-purpose AI-image detectors, including the compared methods, were never used to select the 407 images. Generation-platform watermark tests served as provenance evidence. Clearly artistic AI creations, extremely blurred or low-resolution files, severely truncated images without a fuller source, and platform-added AI labels without a clean version were excluded during manual screening. Evidence is not infallible; corrections can be reported by sample ID.

The 407 images span 320 stored split groups. These are related-sample bookkeeping groups, not 320 independently verified events. Fact-check/report sources are a collection bias; complete propagation histories and per-image degradation types are not inferred.
